---
title: "Evidence-Aligned Vision–Language Explanations for Chest Radiograph Diagnosis"
subtitle: "Training explanation grounding against classifier evidence and testing it counterfactually across open multi-institution data"
---

**Abstract.** Vision–language models write fluent chest radiograph findings, but nothing in their training requires the regions a sentence refers to to be the regions that drove the diagnosis. EviAlign-CXR trains a multi-label classifier jointly with an explanation decoder and constrains the two to agree on evidence. The classifier uses a class-query attention head whose logits decompose exactly into per-patch contributions. For every finding asserted in a report sentence, a Jensen–Shannon term pulls the decoder's token-to-patch grounding towards the stop-gradient evidence map of that finding. An instance-level contrastive term stops a population-average location from satisfying the constraint, an anatomical term penalises evidence outside the lung and heart fields, and a sufficiency–comprehensiveness term ties the maps to the classifier's response under blurred perturbation. Faithfulness is tested with two counterfactual interventions: text-guided deletion removes the region a sentence names and measures the classifier's drop, and explanation counterfactual sensitivity removes a finding's evidence and measures the fall in the likelihood of the sentence asserting it. Because the sufficiency–comprehensiveness term trains the classifier to respond to blurred removal, both tests were repeated with Gaussian-noise infill that training never used and were read through matched ablations. The model was trained on 98,770 adult studies from ReXGradient-160K and evaluated without adaptation on NIH ChestX-ray14, ChestX-Det, RSNA Pneumonia, IU X-ray, Shenzhen, Montgomery and TBX11K. Macro-AUROC equalled that of a classifier-only model (NIH 0.804 for both; paired difference 0.000, 95% CI −0.003 to 0.003). The classifier's evidence located findings better than Grad-CAM (pointing-game hit 0.58 against 0.30 on NIH and 0.73 against 0.34 on ChestX-Det), and sentence grounding followed it (0.63 and 0.74), whereas the grounding of an unguided decoder fell inside radiologist boxes less often than chance. Under noise infill, removing the region a sentence named lowered the classifier logit by 1.9 more than random removal with alignment and by 0.7 without it (0.4 for the unguided model; reduced protocol, NIH, [R: mean ± s.d. over three seeds]). The decoder-side test did not favour alignment (ECS gap 0.06 against 0.14–0.17 for the unguided model in the full protocol), so attention alignment did not make the wording depend on the evidence. Effects measured with blurred removal were five times larger than under noise and were largely a product of training on that operator. [R: tuberculosis transfer result.]

**Keywords:** chest radiography; vision–language models; explainable AI; faithfulness; natural-language explanation; external validation

# 1. Introduction

Deep classifiers trained on large chest radiograph collections reach high AUROC on internal test splits [@wang2017; @rajpurkar2017; @irvin2019] and lose accuracy at another hospital [@zech2018; @pooch2020; @cohen2020]. Part of the loss comes from shortcuts. Classifiers detect the source institution, portable-film markers and other confounders, and saliency analysis has exposed models whose scores rest on such cues [@zech2018; @degrave2021; @geirhos2020]. A clinician reading the output needs to know where the evidence lies and whether it is the right evidence [@tonekaboni2019]. Post-hoc saliency does not reliably supply that. It often fails localisation and model-randomisation checks on radiographs [@adebayo2018; @arun2021; @zhangj2023], and every method tested on CheXlocalize localised pathology worse than radiologists [@saporta2022]. Ghassemi et al. argue that such explanations should not be trusted for individual decisions without validation [@ghassemi2021].

Vision–language models (VLMs) offer an explanation in the form radiologists already use: a sentence such as "moderate right pleural effusion with adjacent basal atelectasis" carries a finding, a location and a severity. Chest radiograph VLMs range from contrastive encoders [@zhang2022convirt; @huang2021gloria; @boecking2022; @tiu2022; @zhang2023kad] to report generators and generalist assistants [@chen2020r2gen; @tanida2023; @chen2024chexagent; @zambrano2025; @tu2024], and they are trained to reproduce reports. A generated sentence can be fluent and correct while bearing no relation to the image region a co-deployed classifier used. General-domain studies show the same gap between what models say and what drives their outputs [@turpin2023; @lanham2023; @parcalabescu2024], and a causal audit of medical VLM heatmaps found that none of the tested VLMs met basic faithfulness criteria while conventional chest X-ray classifiers did [@sadanandan2026].

This paper asks a narrow question: when a system outputs a diagnosis and a sentence explaining it, can training force the sentence to point at the evidence the diagnosis used, and can that property be measured independently of the training objective? Prior work approaches it from several sides. TieNet and PromptMRG couple classification and report generation [@wang2018tienet; @jin2024promptmrg]; CAMANet supervises a report generator's cross-modal attention with aggregated class activation maps [@wang2024camanet]; explanation-guided training constrains attributions with annotations or priors [@ross2017; @rieger2020; @selvaraju2019hint; @rao2023]; WeNLEX makes chest X-ray explanations faithful through reconstruction in the classifier's feature space [@riotorto2026]. None aligns the grounding of each explanation sentence, finding by finding, with an exact evidence decomposition of the classifier, and none tests the result with counterfactual interventions on held-out institutions.

EviAlign-CXR does both. A class-query attention head decomposes every logit exactly over image patches. A LoRA-adapted language model reads projected visual tokens as a prefix and exposes token-to-patch attention for every generated word. Sentence-level finding detection in the reference report assigns tokens to findings, and a Jensen–Shannon term aligns their grounding with that finding's evidence, with the gradient stopped on the classifier side. An instance-level contrastive term blocks the shortcut of grounding "effusion" to the costophrenic angles of an average radiograph. Anatomical and sufficiency–comprehensiveness terms act on the classifier, the latter with blurred rather than zeroed regions to avoid the distribution shift that compromises removal tests [@hooker2019; @rong2022].

The evaluation is built so that the training losses cannot grade themselves. Evidence–grounding agreement is reported as a consistency statistic because it is optimised. Faithfulness is judged by two interventions that no loss targets directly: removing the region a sentence names (text-guided deletion) and removing a finding's evidence to see whether the sentence changes (explanation counterfactual sensitivity). One training term teaches the classifier to lose score when blurred evidence is removed, which is the operation these tests perform, so the interventions are repeated with a removal operator that training never saw and every claim is read against matched ablations. Training uses ReXGradient-160K, a multi-site US collection with free-text reports [@zhangx2025rexgradient]. Seven open corpora from the United States and China serve as external tests, and because none shares images with training, NIH-derived annotation sets such as ChestX-Det and RSNA Pneumonia become fully external localisation benchmarks. A synthetic study with known lesion locations and a spurious marker, correlated with one finding in training only, isolates the contribution of each term before the clinical results are read.

# 2. Related work

**Classification and its failures across sites.** ChestX-ray14 released 112,120 frontal radiographs with fourteen report-mined labels and a small set of boxes [@wang2017], and CheXNet matched radiologists on pneumonia F1 [@rajpurkar2017]. CheXpert, PadChest and VinDr-CXR added uncertainty-aware labels, Spanish reports and radiologist boxes [@irvin2019; @bustos2020; @nguyen2022], and MIMIC-CXR added reports at scale under credentialed access [@johnson2019]. Visual review found label errors in ChestX-ray14 large enough to question some classes [@oakden2020]. Cohen et al. attribute much of the cross-dataset drop to label definitions that change between sources rather than to image appearance alone [@cohen2020]. Self-supervised encoders such as RAD-DINO now rival language-supervised ones [@perez2025]; its pretraining includes all NIH and PadChest images, which rules it out as a backbone here.

**Saliency and what it can be trusted for.** Class activation mapping showed that a linear head on pooled features localises discriminative regions [@zhou2016cam]; Grad-CAM extended it to arbitrary architectures [@selvaraju2017], and Integrated Gradients derived attributions from axioms [@sundararajan2017]. In transformers, raw attention is a contested explanation [@jain2019; @wiegreffe2019]; rollout, relevance propagation and attention-aware LRP track ablations more closely [@abnar2020; @chefer2021; @achtibat2024], and B-cos networks make attributions exact by construction [@bohle2022]. Evaluation is itself fragile: sanity checks exposed methods insensitive to model parameters [@adebayo2018], ROAR showed many attributions do no better than random under retraining [@hooker2019], and ROAD showed that the shape of removed regions leaks class information [@rong2022]. Deletion and insertion curves [@petsiuk2018] and the pointing game [@zhang2018eb] remain the standard quantitative tests and are used here with blurred infill.

**Vision–language models and explanation-guided training.** Contrastive pretraining on image–report pairs produced label-efficient encoders [@zhang2022convirt; @huang2021gloria; @boecking2022; @bannur2023; @wangz2022medclip; @tiu2022; @wu2023medklip; @zhang2023kad; @zhang2025biomedclip]. GLoRIA learns word-to-region attention through local contrastive alignment, and BioViL released MS-CXR for phrase grounding [@huang2021gloria; @boecking2022]. Generative models moved from memory-driven transformers [@chen2020r2gen] to region-guided description [@tanida2023], grounded reports with a box per sentence [@bannur2024maira2], interactive region description [@muller2024chex] and LLM-based assistants [@chen2024chexagent; @zambrano2025; @tu2024; @li2023llavamed]. ReXGradient-160K and CheXpert Plus supply report-paired data from outside MIMIC [@zhangx2025rexgradient; @chambon2024], ReXrank standardises evaluation [@zhangx2024rexrank], and text is scored with labeler-based F1, RadGraph and RadCliQ, GREEN, and factual-consistency rewards [@smit2020; @jain2021radgraph; @yu2023; @ostmeier2024; @liu2019; @miura2021; @delbrouck2022]. In all of these, grounding is an output or a pretraining signal and is not tied to a diagnostic classifier's evidence. Outside report generation, right-for-the-right-reasons penalises input gradients on regions an expert marks irrelevant [@ross2017]; CDEP, GAIN, attribution priors and saliency-guided training constrain attributions or attention in other ways [@rieger2020; @li2018gain; @erion2021; @ismail2021]; and an energy loss that maximises attribution mass inside boxes works with coarse annotations and helps under shift [@rao2023]. In VQA, HINT and self-critical reasoning align gradient importance with human attention [@selvaraju2019hint; @wu2019]. Radiology variants supervise attention with anatomy mentions mined from reports or with lesion boxes [@yu2022agxnet; @luo2022]; reviews are in [@friedrich2023; @gao2024]. Viviano et al. found that correcting attributions improved generalisation but that the size of the correction did not predict the size of the gain [@viviano2021], which is why this paper keeps what the losses optimise apart from what the evaluation measures.

**Faithfulness of natural-language explanations.** e-SNLI and e-ViL set up explanation benchmarks [@camburu2018; @kayser2021], Park et al. showed that textual justifications also improve evidence localisation [@park2018], and MIMIC-NLE brought the task to chest radiographs [@kayser2022]. Faithfulness, as distinct from plausibility, is the degree to which an explanation reflects the reasoning behind a prediction [@jacovi2020]. It has been tested with counterfactual input edits [@atanasova2023; @siegel2024] and contribution-based self-consistency such as CC-SHAP [@parcalabescu2024]. Chain-of-thought studies found that models change answers in response to cues their explanations never mention [@turpin2023; @lanham2023], and in medical VQA explanation quality and answer accuracy came apart under perturbation [@moll2025].

**Position of this work.** The closest systems are CAMANet, which aligns a report generator's attention with class-agnostic aggregated CAMs [@wang2024camanet]; PromptMRG and TieNet, which couple classifier and generator without spatial alignment [@jin2024promptmrg; @wang2018tienet]; and WeNLEX [@riotorto2026]. EviAlign-CXR differs from CAMANet in four respects that the experiments isolate: alignment is per finding and per sentence, the classifier evidence is an exact decomposition rather than a post-hoc CAM, the alignment has an instance-level contrastive component, and faithfulness is evaluated by intervention. A CAMANet-style aggregated alignment is included as an ablation within the same architecture.
